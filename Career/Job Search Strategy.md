## Overview
Job searching is a strategic process, not random applications. Think of it as 戦略 (せんりゃく - strategy) - you need intelligence, planning, and tactical execution to succeed.

## The Modern Job Market Reality

### Hidden Job Market
**80% of jobs are never publicly advertised**
- Internal promotions and transfers
- Employee referrals  
- Direct recruitment/headhunting
- Network connections
- Company websites before job boards

### Application Statistics
- **Average applications per hire**: 250+
- **Interviews per hire**: 4-6 candidates
- **Response rate**: 2-3% for online applications
- **Referral success rate**: 6-10x higher than cold applications

**Takeaway**: Quality over quantity, and networking beats applying blind.

## Research-Driven Job Search

### Company Research Framework

#### Tier 1: Basic Intelligence
- **Company size** - Startup, mid-size, enterprise?
- **Industry position** - Market leader, challenger, niche player?
- **Recent news** - Funding, acquisitions, product launches
- **Company culture** - Values, work environment, team structure
- **Growth trajectory** - Expanding, stable, restructuring?

#### Tier 2: Deep Dive
- **Financial health** - Revenue trends, profitability, investor backing
- **Leadership team** - Background of executives and managers
- **Technology stack** - Tools and platforms they use
- **Competitors** - Who they compete against, market positioning
- **Challenges** - Industry pressures, regulatory issues, market shifts

#### Tier 3: Insider Knowledge
- **Employee reviews** - Glassdoor, LinkedIn insights
- **Interview experiences** - Glassdoor interview section
- **Network connections** - LinkedIn 2nd/3rd degree connections
- **Social media presence** - How they engage online
- **Recent hires** - LinkedIn shows new team members

### Research Tools and Sources

#### Free Resources
```
🔍 Company Intelligence:
• LinkedIn Company Pages - Team, updates, culture
• Glassdoor - Reviews, salaries, interview experiences  
• Crunchbase - Funding, leadership, company metrics
• Google News - Recent developments and coverage
• Company blog/news section - Direct insights

🏢 Industry Analysis:
• Industry reports (free summaries)
• Trade publications and websites
• Professional associations
• Government databases and statistics
```

#### Advanced Research Techniques
```python
# LinkedIn Advanced Search Strategy
```

### Competitive Analysis
**Study your competition** (other candidates):
- What backgrounds do current employees have?
- What skills appear most frequently in job postings?
- What certifications or education are preferred?
- How can you differentiate yourself?

### Referral Strategy

#### Building Your Referral Network
1. **Map your current network**
   - Former colleagues and classmates
   - Industry contacts and mentors
   - Alumni associations
   - Professional organization members

2. **Expand strategically**
   - LinkedIn networking (warm introductions)
   - Industry events and meetups
   - Online communities and forums
   - Volunteer work and side projects

3. **Nurture relationships**
   - Regular check-ins (quarterly coffee chats)
   - Share relevant industry articles
   - Offer help before asking for favors
   - Celebrate others' achievements

#### The Referral Ask
```
❌ Poor approach:
"Hi! I'm looking for a job. Can you refer me to your company?"

✅ Strong approach:
"Hi Sarah! Hope you're doing well. I've been following [Company]'s work on [specific project/initiative] and I'm really impressed with [specific detail]. I see they have an opening for [role], which aligns perfectly with my experience in [relevant area]. Would you be open to a quick chat about the team culture and role expectations? I'd love to learn more from your perspective."
```

## Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) Mastery

### How ATS Systems Work
1. **Parsing**: Extracts text from your CV/resume
2. **Keyword Matching**: Compares your content to job requirements
3. **Scoring**: Assigns relevance score based on matches
4. **Ranking**: Orders candidates by score
5. **Human Review**: Top-scored applications reach recruiters

### ATS Optimization Strategy

#### Keyword Research
```
Job Description Analysis:
1. Copy job posting into word cloud generator
2. Identify repeated keywords and phrases
3. Note required vs. preferred qualifications  
4. Match technical terms exactly (Python not python)
5. Include both acronyms and full terms (AI, Artificial Intelligence)
```

#### CV Formatting for ATS
**ATS-Friendly Format:**
```
CONTACT INFORMATION
Name
Phone | Email | LinkedIn | Location

PROFESSIONAL SUMMARY  
2-3 lines highlighting value proposition with keywords

CORE COMPETENCIES
• Keyword 1 • Keyword 2 • Keyword 3
• Keyword 4 • Keyword 5 • Keyword 6

PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE
Job Title | Company | Dates
• Achievement with quantifiable result
• Keywords naturally integrated into accomplishments

EDUCATION
Degree | Institution | Year
Relevant coursework, honors, certifications
```

#### Common ATS Killers
- ❌ Tables and text boxes
- ❌ Headers/footers with contact info
- ❌ Images, logos, graphics
- ❌ Unusual fonts or formatting
- ❌ Special characters and symbols
- ❌ Multiple columns

## Application Strategy

### Quality vs. Quantity Approach

#### The 80/20 Rule
- **80% effort**: 20% of applications (target companies, perfect matches)
- **20% effort**: 80% of applications (broader opportunities, practice)

#### Customization Levels

**Level 1: Full Customization (Top 20%)**
- Company-specific cover letter
- CV tailored to job requirements  
- Research-based application
- Network connection identified
- Follow-up strategy planned

**Level 2: Moderate Customization (Next 30%)**
- Template cover letter with company details
- Minor CV adjustments
- Basic company research
- Standard application process

**Level 3: Minimal Customization (Remaining 50%)**
- Generic cover letter
- Standard CV
- Quick applications for practice and pipeline building

## Recruitment Agencies and Headhunters

### Types of Recruiters

#### Internal Recruiters
- **Work for**: Specific company
- **Focus**: That company's open roles
- **Best for**: Targeted applications to specific companies
- **Relationship**: One-time interaction usually

#### External Recruiters
- **Work for**: Recruitment agency  
- **Focus**: Multiple clients and roles
- **Best for**: Market exploration and options
- **Relationship**: Ongoing partnership potential

#### Executive Search (Headhunters)
- **Work for**: Senior-level placements
- **Focus**: Passive candidates, leadership roles
- **Best for**: Senior positions ($100K+)
- **Relationship**: Long-term career partnerships

### Working with Recruiters Effectively

#### Initial Contact
```
✅ Professional Response:
"Thank you for reaching out about the [Role] opportunity. I'm interested in learning more. I'm currently [brief status] and exploring opportunities that [align with your goals]. Would you be available for a brief call this week to discuss the role and my background?"

❌ Poor Response:
"Send me the job description and I'll let you know if I'm interested."
```

#### Building Recruiter Relationships
1. **Be responsive** - Reply within 24-48 hours
2. **Be honest** about your situation and interests
3. **Provide feedback** after interviews they arrange
4. **Stay in touch** even when not actively searching
5. **Refer others** when appropriate

## Timeline and Process Management

### Typical Job Search Timeline

#### Preparation Phase (2-4 weeks)
- [ ] Update CV and LinkedIn profile
- [ ] Develop target company list
- [ ] Research industry salary ranges
- [ ] Prepare portfolio/work samples
- [ ] Practice interview responses

#### Active Search Phase (8-12 weeks)
- [ ] Submit 5-10 applications per week
- [ ] Network actively (2-3 conversations per week)
- [ ] Follow up on applications (1-2 weeks after submission)
- [ ] Interview preparation and execution
- [ ] Maintain pipeline of opportunities

#### Decision Phase (2-4 weeks)
- [ ] Evaluate offers against criteria
- [ ] Negotiate terms and compensation  
- [ ] Make final decision
- [ ] Notify other opportunities
- [ ] Plan transition from current role

### Managing Multiple Opportunities

#### Pipeline Management
```
Early Stage (10-15 opportunities):
• Applications submitted
• Initial conversations
• Research and networking

Middle Stage (5-8 opportunities):  
• Phone/video screenings
• Take-home assignments
• Recruiter discussions

Late Stage (2-3 opportunities):
• Final round interviews
• Reference checks
• Offer negotiations
```

```
Benefits to Consider:

🏥 Health: Medical, dental, vision, mental health

📈 Growth: Training budget, conference attendance, tuition
```

## Rejection Management and Resilience

### Learning from Rejections
**Request feedback** (even if you don't always get it):
```
"Thank you for letting me know about your decision. While I'm disappointed, I respect your choice. If possible, I'd appreciate any feedback about areas where I could strengthen my candidacy for similar roles in the future. I remain interested in [Company] and would welcome the opportunity to be considered for future positions."
```

### Maintaining Momentum
- **Track metrics** - Applications, responses, interviews
- **Celebrate small wins** - Interviews scheduled, positive feedback
- **Stay organized** - Consistent daily/weekly activities
- **Seek support** - Career groups, mentors, friends
- **Keep learning** - Use downtime for skill development

### Red Flags to Watch For
```
🚩 Company Red Flags:
• Vague job descriptions
• Unrealistic salary ranges  
• High employee turnover
• Poor online reviews consistently
• Unprofessional interview process
• Pressure to accept immediately

🚩 Recruiter Red Flags:
• Requests personal information upfront
• Vague about client company
• Pushes you toward unsuitable roles
• Poor communication or responsiveness
• Requests exclusive representation without value
```
