
### Don't list
When writing experiences dont list your activities or duties. These mean little to the recrioter as they wont know what those duties actuallu involved or what you got out of thwm. Desctibe the results you achieved and the skills or qualities you demonstrated. 


### Use verbs at the start of the sentence
Start a sentence with a verb or power word. This provides a strong and clear opener and make your skills and achievements stand out, especially when the recruiter is speed reading your application. It also stimulates the reader to want to learn more about you.


### Write in an active style 
An active style puts the emphasis on the action rather than what you were expected to do -- the latter sets a passive tone which wont impress a recruiter. Example:

**Passive style:** ‘The project required me to research information from various sources. I had to make conclusions and present my findings in a report. It involved working under pressure and coping with deadlines.’

**Active style:** ‘Researched information using various sources including databases and scientific journals. Produced a comprehensive report that detailed my findings which received 76% (equivalent to a ‘distinction’ level grade). Learnt the value of prioritizing tasks for effective time management.’


## Write concisely (short paragraphs or bullet points)

Writing in a "block" can be difficult to read and become a turn off to the recruiter. They might have difficulty extracting the key info. Instead use sub headings along with short paragraphs or bullet points to break up text. 

**Effective wording**

-   You have to use short sentences and get to the point.
-   You need to give examples and evidence to prove your point.
-   This style makes it easier to read and communicate key selling points relevant to the job or course.
