
***Jobs boards, recruitment agencies and larger organizations often use an ‘applicant tracking system’ as a first step in shortlisting CVs or applications. This means that what you write will not be reviewed personally by a recruiter but by a software system that will perform a quick scan for key words and phrases that match the job description, skills and experience that the organization is looking for.


### Use the software to your advantage

Make it clear that you meet every requirements. Check for key words and phrases in the job description or person specification and make sure to include in resume or application. If the job describes a role involving "data analytics", include the term within your experiences and skills with evidence. 


### Here are some tips if you are submitting a CV. Some of them could apply to application forms also.

-   Use standard fonts such as **Arial, Calibri, Georgia, Tahoma or Verdana.
-   Leave out special characters.
-   Standard bullets should be fine but avoid non-standard bullet styles or intricate symbols.
-   **Avoid headers and footers, tables, graphics and logos.
-   Make sure that your **headings are clear**. For example, use simple headings such as ‘Work experience’, rather than ‘Professional expertise’.
-   Provide the **full titles of qualifications** or organizations that you have worked for, along with abbreviations or acronyms. For example, The Chartered Institute of Marketing (CIM) - Certificate in Professional Marketing, 2014 - 2016.
-   Add dates at the end of your qualifications and work experience, rather than at the start. For example, Oxfam Shop, Sheffield - Part-time Sales Assistant, Sept 2016 - Jun 2018.
-   Submit your CV as a word document (.doc, .docx), text (.txt) or rich text (.rtf) file. You can convert it into a PDF file if you are sure it is compatible with the software, or you know it will be read by a person rather than a system.
