# Client Boundaries & Scripts

## Scope Creep Scenarios

### "Can you also fix my printer/remove viruses/recover data?"
**Response:**
> "I focus specifically on PC buying guidance and new computer setup - that keeps my expertise sharp and my rates fair. For [their issue], I'd recommend [local shop]. Happy to help if you need PC consultation in the future though!"

**Why this works:** Positions you as specialist, not handyman.

---

### "Why can't you build it for me for $50?"
**Response:**
> "I totally understand budget constraints. At $50, I wouldn't be able to deliver the quality service I'm known for. If budget is tight, I can point you to some good tutorial videos for DIY, or we could do just the parts list consultation ($100) which includes a build guide you can follow yourself."

**Why this works:** Offers alternative, maintains value, doesn't justify pricing.

---

### "Can't you just skip your prayer break?"
**Response:**
> "I have a prior commitment at that time that I can't move. I'm happy to reschedule for [alternative time] or continue tomorrow. My calendar link has all my available slots."

**Why this works:** No justification needed, boundary stated professionally.

---

### "This list is too expensive, just tell me the cheapest laptop"
**Response:**
> "If budget is the primary concern, I can recommend two paths: a refurbished business-class laptop that'll perform better than new budget models (around $300-400), or a new entry-level option if warranty matters more ($350-500). Both will handle your use case, but let me explain the trade-offs..."

**Why this works:** Acknowledges constraint, gives options with context, stays in consultant role.

---

## Key Boundary Principle
**Never justify, just state boundaries professionally.**

When in doubt: "That's outside my service scope, but I can refer you to [alternative]."
