# Pricing Strategy & Psychology

## Price Points (Proven)

**Service 3 (PC Setup):** $175 flat rate
- Market: Geek Squad $150-200, local shops $100-175
- Your differentiation: Come to them + optimization expertise
- After 10 clients: Consider $200 or tiered ($175 basic, $275 premium)

**Service 1 (Consultation):** $100
- Market: Geek Squad $100-150/hr, Best Buy membership $200/yr
- Your differentiation: No upselling, actual advice
- After 10 clients with testimonials: Raise to $125

**Service 2 (Parts List):** $175
- Market: PCPartPicker forums $100-300, shops $150-250
- Your differentiation: Professional deliverable + follow-up
- After 5 clients: Raise to $225

## Why Odd Numbers Work
- $175 > $150 because it's not a "round discount number" (feels calculated/professional)
- $85 > $80 for same reason
- Psychological: Round numbers = rushed, odd = thoughtful

## First 3 Clients Strategy
**Offer:** 40% off ($150 for setup, $85 for consultation)
**In exchange for:**
- Detailed written testimonial
- Permission to use as case study
- Video testimonial (optional but valuable)

**Why this works:** Social proof > money for first month

## When to Raise Prices
✅ After 10 clients with testimonials
✅ When booking 2+ weeks out
✅ When you're working >15hrs/week consistently

❌ Don't raise if you're still booking same-week

## Flat Rate vs Hourly
**Use flat rate for:**
- PC setup ($175 for 2 hours)
- Parts list ($175 async)

**Why:** Client doesn't watch clock, you profit as you get faster

**Use hourly for:**
- Consultation ($100/hr)

**Why:** Clear value exchange, easier to quote

## The "Free Question" Trap
Every "quick question" text = 15 mins + context switching = $25 value lost

**Solution:** 
> "Happy to help! That's exactly what my consultation service covers. Want to book a time? [calendly link]"
